This is the EarlBear blog. EarlBear is software that runs online storefronts — finding
what is costing a store money, fixing it, testing the fix on real traffic, and keeping
only what works. The posts here are written from inside that system rather than around
it, so they tend to be specific: real numbers, real stores, real experiments that
failed.

If you read nothing else, read the first one below.

## The reading order

1. **[What EarlBear is](/blog/what-is-earlbear)** — the whole thing in one page: four
   agents in a loop, what each one does, and why closing the loop matters more than any
   single stage of it. Start here if you want the shape of the system before the
   details.

2. **[Life without EarlBear](/blog/life-without-earlbear)** — the same work done by
   hand. Every role you would have to hire, 2026 salary bands, how long each kind of
   change takes, and what the whole team costs per month. There is a calculator you can
   adjust to your own traffic and order value.

3. **[A storefront that heals itself](/blog/self-healing-storefront)** — the stage of
   the loop that never stops. How a finding becomes a hypothesis, a hypothesis becomes
   an A/B test on a slice of traffic, and only the winners stay live — under guardrails
   the owner sets.

One is the map, two is the price of not having it, three is the part that compounds.

## What is not here

We also write a lot about how the system is built — the databases, the agents, the
infrastructure the storefront work runs on. Those dispatches live on an internal site
for the engineering team, because they are working notes rather than finished
stories. When one of them turns out to be worth telling properly, it shows up here.
