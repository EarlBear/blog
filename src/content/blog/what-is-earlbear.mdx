EarlBear runs online storefronts the way a senior growth team would — finding the
problems, building the fixes, testing them on real traffic, and keeping only what
works — except it never clocks out. It is not a dashboard you log into and a set of
recommendations you then have to act on. It is a **loop of AI agents** that does the
work.

Each agent has one job and hands off to the next. The last stage feeds the first, so
the system gets better at finding stores by learning what actually lifted revenue on
the ones it already runs.

## What each stage does

**Scan.** Most lead lists are a filter over a database someone else built. EarlBear's
scan crawls the live store and audits it — page speed, the product page, the cart,
the checkout path — and what comes out is not a company name but a store plus the
specific revenue gaps found on it.

**Outreach.** Because the scan produced evidence, the pitch can be about *this*
store: here is what we found, here is what it is likely costing you. Nothing sends
without a human approving it.

**Onboard.** A short structured intake collects brand context, assets, and access,
and validates them, so the first improvement can ship immediately rather than after
three weeks of chasing logins.

**Review.** This is the stage that never stops. The store is scored continuously,
each finding becomes a hypothesis, each hypothesis becomes an experiment on a slice
of traffic, and only changes that measurably beat the control stay live.

## Why a loop, and not a tool

A tool hands you a list. A loop closes it.

The reason that distinction matters is cost. Running the loop by hand means hiring
for every stage of it — the person who finds prospects, the one who writes the
outreach, the one who runs onboarding, the analyst who designs the tests and reads
the results. We priced that team out, role by role, with real 2026 salary bands, in
[life without EarlBear](/blog/life-without-earlbear).

The most interesting stage is the last one, because it is the one owners almost never
run themselves: continuous A/B testing on a live store, with guardrails, without
risking sales. [A storefront that heals
itself](/blog/self-healing-storefront) walks through exactly how that works — how a
finding becomes an experiment, what stops a bad variant, and how the result gets
reported in dollars rather than percentages.
